# 📊 What is Statistics?

<div class="quote-box">
  <p><em>“Statistics is the grammar of science.”</em></p>
  <p class="quote-author">— Karl Pearson</p>
</div>

Statistics (*status rerum* — "state of affairs") is a tool of the scientific method that uses mathematics to study a collective phenomenon.  
This study can be both **qualitative** and **quantitative** and occurs under conditions of **uncertainty** or **non-determinism**, with a partial knowledge of the phenomenon.

The need to quantify the phenomena under study — that is, to analyze and describe them in mathematical terms — has its origins in **ancient Egypt**, 
where successive dynasties introduced the recording of data such as **population size**, **material goods**, **number of soldiers**, and so on.  

Obviously, statistics has undergone significant evolution over the ages, up to the present day where two main branches are recognized: **descriptive** and **inferential** statistics.
Both are fundamental in transforming **data into knowledge**, providing the mathematical foundation of decision-making under uncertainty.

---

## 📈 Main Branches of Statistics

### 1️⃣ Descriptive Statistics
Its purpose is to **summarize data** through graphical tools (such as bar charts, pie charts, histograms, box plots) and indices 
(statistical indicators, position indicators, dispersion indicators, correlation indicators, shape indicators, etc.) that describe 
the salient aspects of the observed data, thus forming the statistical content.

### 2️⃣ Inferential Statistics
Its purpose is to **establish the characteristics** of the data and the behavior of the measurements collected (statistical variables) 
with a predetermined **probability of error**. It is strongly connected to **probability theory** and 
allows us to make predictions based on models derived from inferential techniques.

## 🔐 Statistics and Cybersecurity

**Cybersecurity** isn't just at the top of the IT agenda. Businesses and governments around the world are wary of growing **cyber threats**, 
and many have learned the hard way that **SMBs** (Small and Medium-sized Businesses) are often the target of cyber attacks.

A **CISCO** study reveals that 40% of SMBs that suffered a cyberattack experienced at least eight hours of **downtime**. 
Downtime accounts for a large portion of the overall **financial damage** from a security breach.

Statistics plays a crucial role in modern cybersecurity.  
The large amount of data generated by networks, systems, and users can be analyzed statistically to detect anomalies, recognize patterns, and prevent attacks.

Some key applications include:

- **Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS)** → Statistical models help identify unusual network traffic by comparing it with normal behavioral baselines.  
- **Malware Analysis** → Statistical classification techniques can distinguish malicious software from legitimate programs based on code characteristics.  
- **User Behavior Analytics (UBA)** → Using probabilistic models and statistical distributions, systems can detect deviations from typical user actions that may indicate compromised accounts.  
- **Cryptanalysis** → In the field of cryptography, statistics supports the study of ciphertext distributions and helps in the detection of weak or non-random encryption schemes.  
- **Risk Assessment** → Statistical inference is used to estimate the probability and impact of potential security incidents, allowing the prioritization of defenses.

In short, **statistics transforms raw data into actionable intelligence**, enabling proactive defense strategies and improving the reliability of decision-making processes in cybersecurity.
